# Praśna Upaniṣad — how the text is settled

**Source of truth:** shlokam.org, "Prashnopanishad" (<https://shlokam.org/text/prashnopanishad.htm>), a page the owner allowed, saved as HTML (private copy in scriptures-data at `hinduism/veda/atharvaveda/upanishad/prashna-upanishad/printouts/shlokam/prashnopanishad.html`). It gives the śānti mantra and the six praśnas, mantra by mantra, in Devanagari without svaras, each with the page's transliteration, English and Śaṅkara's commentary in S. Sitarama Sastri's English. Each praśna's closing words are printed as the last line of its last mantra.

## The witnesses
- **The page's own transliteration.** `source/settle.py` converts it back to Devanagari and compares it with the page's Devanagari word by word. They agree in every word of all 68 blocks: the transliteration is made from the same Devanagari, so it confirms the page's encoding (no letter is lost or broken in the HTML), and its slips are the Devanagari's slips too. It is not an independent edition, so it never outvotes the grammar.
- **Śaṅkara's commentary as the page gives it** (English): it renders the words it explains, and confirms the reading where a slip leaves a non-word (below).
- **The Māṇḍūkya's settled śānti** (`tools/works/mandukya-upanishad/`): the same śānti mantra of the Atharvaveda's Upaniṣads.
- **Five web texts**, each written as a Devanagari text in `source/witness/<site>.txt` by `source/witnesses.py` from its raw download (in scriptures-data at `<work path>/printouts/<site>/`, each with its `.meta.json`):
  - **upanishads.org.in** (`/u/prashna/p/<praśna.mantra>`, the Sanskrit of each page's `p.sloka`): all 67 mantras, no śānti; mantra 1.1 opens with ॐ नमः परमात्मने । हरिः ॐ ।, the site's invocation, not part of the mantra. Its lines are the base page's lines in 65 of the 67 mantras and it shares the page's readings where the others differ (सर्व in 2.4, तदेषः in 3.11, सौर्यायणि in 4.1, यदैतस्मिन् in 4.6, कौशल्य in 3.1): the same text as the base, with its slips corrected.
  - **Sanskrit Wikisource**, प्रश्न उपनिषद् (`action=raw`): the śānti before and after, the closing words of each praśna, 67 mantras (its 3.4 has no number; `witnesses.py` ends it at its blank line). It has nearly every slip of the base page (कुते, प्रण, सम्राद्, एतन् ग्रामानोतान्, सोभ्य, वसोवृक्षं, यादौ, विभुति, नार्हम्यनृतं, यस्मिन्नताः, यथ …) and more of its own: the base page's own text.
  - **Vedic Heritage Portal** (vedicheritage.gov.in, `prashnopanishad/`, the page's text beside its video): the śānti before and after, the closing words, 67 mantras. It shares many of the base page's slips (वसोवृक्षं, यादौ, तथासङ्कल्पितं, सौर्यायणि, तदेषः, कौशल्य in 3.1) and has its own: a copy of the same text, corrected in places (सर्वा in 2.4, सम्राड्). The page writes a private-use character (U+E001) for the anusvāra in यजूंषि and तुष्टुवांसः; `witnesses.py` reads it as ं.
  - **IIT Kanpur**, Heritage reader, text PRU (the primary moolam of each verse): no śānti before the text (it prints one after 6.8, not compared). Its 72 refs hold the 67 mantras with copies: its 4.1.12 repeats 3.12 (ending इति तृतीयः प्रश्नः) and its 5.1.8–5.1.11 repeat 4.8–4.11 (ending इति चतुर्थः प्रश्नः), so its praśnas 4 and 5 count 12 and 11 refs for 11 and 7 mantras; its 1.1.5 and 1.1.7 hold 1.6 and 1.8 again, so it lacks 1.5 and 1.7. The copies are left out (`witnesses.py` → `REPEATS`, `check.py` → `ABSENT`). An independent text, with many typing slips of its own (रू for रु, ध्द for द्ध, ष for ऋष, a nukta under श, …), so it counts only where it agrees with GRETIL or the grammar.
  - **GRETIL**, `sa_praznopaniSad-comm.xml` (the mūla with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya, in IAST; GRETIL's header: "Apparent errors have been silently corrected"): the mūla of each "START PrUp n.m" block, converted to Devanagari with `settle.py`'s transliteration code (`tr2deva_word`), a word's final consonant joined to the next word as GRETIL writes them apart (śaibyaś ca, pippalādam upasannāḥ). It writes a word in pausa at its daṇḍas (प्रजापतिः । तस्य, इति । एष), which `check.py` joins by external sandhi; three of its blocks carry words of the bhāṣya (1.12, 2.9, 6.5). An independent text, the one Śaṅkara comments on; its bhāṣya, which quotes the words it explains, is read too.

The base page, Wikisource, the Vedic Heritage Portal and upanishads.org.in are one text in four copies (PLAYBOOK §2): their agreement is one witness. IIT Kanpur and GRETIL (with Śaṅkara's bhāṣya) are the independent witnesses. `source/check.py` compares every praśna of `text.txt` with every witness as one run of words (IIT Kanpur divides praśnas 4 and 5 with its copies), treating spacing, the standard's nasals, the anusvāra for a doubled न or म, the avagraha and words written apart in pausa as conventions; it finds **no difference** apart from those listed in its `READING`, each of which is one of the classes below.

The library's writing standard (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §3) applies: ॥ and compact verse numbers, Pāṇini's spelling, the ISO 15919 transliteration made from the verse, ं for ꣳ outside the Yajurveda.

## The text as read
- **Zero-width characters.** The page puts a joiner inside श‍ृ (शृणुयाम in the śānti; शृणोति, अनुशृणोति in 4.2 and 4.5); `settle.py` removes it, as the Māṇḍūkya does, and removes any zero-width space.
- **Numbers.** The page ends each mantra ॥ १.१॥ (praśna.mantra). The text ends it with the mantra's own number, ॥१॥, and cites it praśna.mantra (`work.json` → `cite`), as the Kaṭha does.
- **Closing words.** The page prints इति प्रश्नोपनिषदि प्रथमः प्रश्नः ॥ (… षष्ठः प्रश्नः) as the last line of each praśna's last mantra. Each is an item of its own, `1.iti` … `6.iti`, of type `iti`, in its praśna's section; the words are the page's (Wikisource and the Vedic Heritage Portal word them the same; IIT Kanpur writes इति प्रथमः प्रश्नः; they are not compared).
- **Line breaks** are the page's, but two: 5.5 breaks अभि-ध्यायीत across two lines (the text keeps the word whole: पुरुषमभिध्यायीत at the end of the first line); 6.4 prints its number on a line of its own (it joins the line before).
- **ꣳ** in तुष्टुवांसस् (śānti) and यजूंषि (2.6): the page writes ꣳ, the Yajurveda's sign; this is the Atharvaveda, so ं.

## The śānti mantra
The page's śānti is the Māṇḍūkya's settled śānti (भद्रं कर्णेभिः … with the four स्वस्ति lines), word for word, but for one slip: स्थिरैरङ्गैस्तुष्**त**ुवाꣳसस्, a non-word (after ष the dental त becomes ट, Pāṇini 8.4.41; the verb is stu, तुष्टुवांसः), here as in its transliteration (tuṣtuvā-), Wikisource and the Vedic Heritage Portal; GRETIL has tuṣṭuvāṃsas; the text has तुष्टुवांसस्, as the Māṇḍūkya. The page's eight short lines are laid out in the Māṇḍūkya's five, and यदायुः ends with । as there, so the two Upaniṣads show the same mantra the same way; `settle.py` checks that the two are equal. GRETIL's पश्येमाक्षिभिः (for अक्षभिः, the Vedic form all the others have) and its lack of the closing ॐ शान्तिः; the Vedic Heritage Portal's देवाः and तार्क्ष्योऽरिष्टनेमिः (the sandhi the Māṇḍūkya's text leaves open): the base stays.

## The witnesses' readings, where the base stays
A valid reading of the base stays (PLAYBOOK §2), here against one or both independent witnesses:
- **2.4 the verbs.** The page: सोऽभिमानादूर्ध्वमुत्क्रामत इव … सर्व एव प्रतिष्ठन्ते … सर्वा एवोत्क्रमन्ते … सर्वा एव प्रातिष्ठन्त. IIT Kanpur and GRETIL: उत्क्रमत, प्रातिष्ठन्ते, उत्क्रामन्ते, and प्रतिष्ठन्त (IIT Kanpur) or प्रातिष्ठन्ते (GRETIL). Śaṅkara's bhāṣya quotes उत्क्रामत इव, as the page, and सर्व एव प्रातिष्ठन्ते, उत्क्रामन्ते, प्रतिष्ठन्ते. Each of the page's forms is a form of the same verb (प्रतिष्ठन्ते the present and प्रातिष्ठन्त the imperfect of प्रति-स्था; उत्क्रामत and उत्क्रमत, उत्क्रामन्ते and उत्क्रमन्ते of उत्-क्रम्, the Upaniṣad using both stems), so each stays.
- **3.1 प्रतिष्ठते, 3.5 प्रातिष्ठते**: IIT Kanpur, GRETIL and Śaṅkara read प्रातिष्ठते in 3.1 and प्रतिष्ठते in 3.5. Both are forms the Upaniṣad uses; the page's stay.
- **3.9 तेजो ह वा उदानः**: IIT Kanpur and the Vedic Heritage Portal वाव; GRETIL as the page. The base stays.
- **2.8 पितॄणां**: IIT Kanpur, Wikisource and the Vedic Heritage Portal पितृणां; the page, GRETIL and upanishads.org.in पितॄणां, the genitive plural of पितृ. The base stays.
- **Sandhi at a pause**: IIT Kanpur and the Vedic Heritage Portal join across the page's pauses (2.3 मोहमापद्यथा अहमेवैतत्, मोहमापद्यथाहम्; 4.4 यजमान इष्टफलम्), GRETIL across 6.4 मनः । अन्नम् (मनो ऽन्नम्); GRETIL writes 5.5 विनिर्मुच्यते and IIT Kanpur, GRETIL and the Vedic Heritage Portal 3.8 पुरुषस्यापानम् where the page has विनिर्मुच्यत एवं and पुरुषस्य अपानम्. The same words; the page's writing stays.
- **Spellings**: 3.1 अस्मिञ्शरीर (IIT Kanpur ञ्छ, optional by 8.4.63; GRETIL अस्मिन्शरीरे, the sandhi left open); 2.11 एकर्षि (GRETIL एकऋषि).

## Kept as the page has it
Where the page's reading is a valid form, it stays, though some printed editions differ:
- तान्ह स ऋषिरुवाच (1.2), उदयन्यत्प्राचीं (1.6): words joined in writing, as the page joins them.
- मनोकृतेनायात्यस्मिञ्शरीरे (3.3): मनोकृतेन is the Upaniṣad's own (Vedic) form, which the commentary explains as "by the act of the mind"; ञ्श, not ञ्छ (छ for श after न् is optional, 8.3.31 with 8.4.63); IIT Kanpur and GRETIL read the same.
- प्रातिष्ठते (3.5), नेयायते (4.2), मातरिश्व (2.11), परमऋषिभ्यो (6.8): Vedic forms, as printed (GRETIL and IIT Kanpur have नेयायते, मातरिश्व and परमऋषिभ्यो too).
- सर्व एवोत्क्रामन्ते … सर्वा एवोत्क्रमन्ते (2.4): both forms of the verb, as printed.
- पुरिशयं (5.5): as printed, and as IIT Kanpur and GRETIL.

## Divisions
The page divides the text into six "chapters", the six praśnas; they are the six parts, each one section. The topics are the śānti pāṭha and one per praśna, under our own names (the question each praśna answers). The page's mantra numbers, restarting in each praśna, are the text's; every witness numbers them the same (16, 13, 12, 11, 7, 8), IIT Kanpur's copies aside.
