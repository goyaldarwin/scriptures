# Reconciliation: श्री ब्रह्मा चालीसा

## Witnesses
- **The book (base):** ५१ चालीसा संग्रह आरतियों सहित (Randhir Prakashan, Haridwar, first edition 2000), pp. 48–52; scriptures-data `hinduism/chalisa/books/aarti-chalisa-sangrah/` (page images i-047 … i-051), read on the page images. Two opening dohās and 53 chaupāīs; no closing dohā (the āratī that follows on p. 53 is a separate work).
- **Drik Panchang** (`hinduism/chalisa/brahma/brahma-chalisa/printouts/drikpanchang/shri-brahma-chalisa.html`): a typed copy of the same text, with 52 chaupāīs: it joins the first half of the book's chaupāī 35 (शंख चक्र अरु गदा मनोहर) to the second half of chaupāī 36 (शेष नाग शय्या अति मनहर), losing पद्म सहित आयुध सब सुन्दर and पायँ पलोटति रमा निरन्तर. The book's two chaupāīs are kept (Shubh Panchang has both lines too). Its own slips: महापघ, श्यायमवर्ण, भ्ौटहु, प्राचीन.
- **For a doubtful reading only:** Shubh Panchang (shubhpanchang.com, Brahma Chalisa). Sites that copy one another count as one witness.

The rule: keep the book; correct it only where it is clearly wrong, and only on the witnesses. The library's writing standard (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §3): a chaupāī's two halves on one line, `।` between them and `॥` at its end; a dohā's two lines, each ending `।` and `॥`; the book's commas are not kept.

|---|---|---|---|
| chaupāī 1 | जय जय कमलासान जगमूला | जय जय कमलासन जगमूला | कमलासान is not a word: the lotus-seated one, कमलासन, as the book itself prints it in chaupāīs 11 and 15; the long ā gives the half 9 mātrās where the chaupāī has 8 (17 in all). Drik Panchang and Shubh Panchang print the same slip; nothing in them supports it. |
| dohā 2 | अज विधि घाता नाम | अज विधि धाता नाम | घाता is no name of Brahmā (Platts: घाता, a thing thrown in over a purchase; घात, slaughter); the line lists his names, and अज, विधि and धाता stand together among them in the Amarakośa (1.1.16–17: धाताब्जयोनिर्द्रुहिणो … विधाता विश्वसृड्विधिः), धाता "creator, sustainer, a name of Brahmā" in Monier-Williams, Apte and Platts. घ for ध is a one-stroke slip that Drik Panchang and Shubh Panchang, typed from the same text, carry over. |
| chaupāī 10 | वीणा वादिनि सब विधि मुन्दर | वीणा वादिनि सब विधि सुन्दर | मुन्दर is not a word: सुन्दर, rhyming with मनोहर; Shubh Panchang सुन्दर (Drik Panchang has the book's slip). |
| chaupāī 29 | भैटहु जाइ | भैंटहु जाइ | भैटहु has lost its anusvāra: the verb is भेंटना, "to meet"; Drik Panchang's भ्ौटहु is garbled and gives no other form. |

Kept as the book has them, against Drik Panchang: chaupāīs 35–36 (above); रूप, जटाजूट, महापद्म, प्राचीना, कहँ कोइ नाहीं, सब कछु, निज इच्छा उन सब (Drik इन सब), श्यामवर्ण, शिवशङ्कर, मांहीं, होइ, अगुणरूप श्री ब्रह्म बखानहु (Drik ब्रह्मा). In chaupāī 39 a stray dot over दोउ is a speck, not an anusvāra. The book and Drik Panchang also agree on सरस्वती तब सुता (तब for तव), गरब बढ़ायो / सुखपाये and करिहै.

## Readings settled
- chalisa.do2 — घाता or धाता among Brahmā's names: धाता, a text correction (the table above: Amarakośa 1.1.16–17, Monier-Williams, Apte, Platts); the meaning already read Dhātā.
- chalisa.cau9 — what हिये does in अपर नाम हिये गायत्री: the plain reading stays, "whose other name, dwelling in the heart, is Gāyatrī" (हिये, "in the heart", Platts हिय); no witness has another word (Drik Panchang and Shubh Panchang print हिये), so no correction.
- chalisa.cau20 — the sense of तबहीं मोहि करयो यह धारन: "for this reason alone: that it bears me" stays; मोहि is the object, the lotus the subject of करयो (it bore me), so "I formed a conviction" would need a subject मैं; Brahmā's conclusion follows in cau21–22 (यह निश्चय करि).
- chalisa.cau40 — सब in ब्रह्मरूप सब त्रिभुवन मांहीं: "we all (three)", meaning changed. Viṣṇu has just said ब्रह्मरूप हम दोउ (cau39), names Śiva as the third (तीजे), and goes on to the three's tasks (cau41–42) and हम साकार रूप त्रयदेवा (cau44): सब gathers the three, not everything in the worlds.
- chalisa.cau46 — what सो सब refers to: "all that", Brahmā's praise of the Supreme Brahman just sung (cau45), known as the Veda, stays; सब fits a body of praise, not one person, and the plain words give it.
