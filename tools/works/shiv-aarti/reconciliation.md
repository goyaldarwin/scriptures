# Reconciliation: ॐ जय शिव ओंकारा

## Source
The text is Anuradha Paudwal's version, as she sings it in the recording the owner chose ("Shree Shiv Aarti", YouTube `Yb2GMwJyGnE`): the refrain, six stanzas, the closing stanza with the author's name, and the refrain once more. Every line is taken from a written witness; where the witnesses differ, the line is the one she sings, decided by the owner by ear.

Written witnesses (private copies in `scriptures-data` at `hinduism/aarti/shiva/shiv-aarti/`):
- **S**: a screenshot of a web page the owner shared (the site is not named).
- **D**: Drikpanchang, "Shiva Aarti" (the owner's saved page, `printouts/drikpanchang/`): the long version, twelve stanzas.
- **B**: Bhakti Bharat, "शिव आरती - ॐ जय शिव ओंकारा" (<https://www.bhaktibharat.com/aarti/shri-shiv-shankar-bholenath>), text given by the owner.
- **BD**: Bharat Discovery, "शिव जी की आरती" (<https://bharatdiscovery.org/india/शिव_जी_की_आरती>), text given by the owner: the closest to her singing, and the base of the text.

The library's writing standard (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §3): ।/॥ for the daṇḍas, stanzas numbered (॥१॥); the refrain (टेक) shown once at the start and once at the end, as she sings it, although it is also sung after each stanza.

## Meanings
Our own, one per verse. The āratī praises Śiva as Oṅkāra, the one in whom Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Śiva are one; stanzas 1–5 name the three in turn (face, seat, arms, garland, garment, companions, what they hold, and their work), and the meanings say which belongs to whom. The word धारा ("bore") has that sense in this work only (`glossary/hi.json`).
