# Reconciliation: नामु तेरो आरती मजनु मुरारे

## Witnesses
- **The book (base):** ५१ चालीसा संग्रह आरतियों सहित (Randhir Prakashan, Haridwar, 2000), p. 94; scriptures-data `hinduism/chalisa/books/aarti-chalisa-sangrah/` (page image i-093), read on the page image. It prints nine lines, each halved by a comma, with no stanza breaks, no refrain cue and no numbers.
- **Drik Panchang** (`hinduism/aarti/ravidas/ravidas-aarti/printouts/drikpanchang/shri-ravidas-aarti.html`): the same nine lines in the same words, in four groups: the first three lines (नामु तेरो आरती … चारे), then two lines each (नाम तेरा दीवा …; नाम तेरो तागा …; दस अठा …).

The rule: keep the book; correct it only where it is clearly wrong. The library's writing standard (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §3): an āratī's verse has no comma (the book's comma between the halves of a line becomes a line break, a half per line); the refrain (टेक) is shown once; stanzas numbered (॥१॥).

| Place | Book | Ours | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| every line | one printed line, its halves joined by a comma | two lines, ending । (and ॥ / ॥N॥ at a verse's end) | The standard; the comma marks the half. |
| the stanzas | nine lines, no breaks | the refrain (line 1) and four stanzas of two printed lines each | The book does not divide the lines. Drik Panchang's groups are pairs of lines after the first, and its first group is one line longer: that line, नामु तेरो आरती मजनु मुरारे, is the theme the pada returns to and the āratī's title, so it is the refrain and the four couplets after it are the stanzas (the last with the poet's name). |
| stanza numbers | none | ॥१॥ … ॥४॥ | The standard. |
| stanza 4, line 3 | कहै 'रविदास' | कहै रविदास | The quotation marks are the printer's, not part of the verse. |
| refrain, line 1 | भजनु | मजनु | A misprint (भ for म): the āratī is Guru Ravidās's hymn in the Guru Granth Sahib (Rāg Dhanāsarī, Ang 694), whose refrain reads ਮਜਨੁ, "ablution, holy bath" (Platts मज्जन; Monier-Williams majjana), as iGurbani (igurbani.com, shabad htjs) and SikhNet (Gurbani Kirtan, tr_1489, "naam tayro aartee majan muraaray") give it. Drik Panchang repeats the book's भजनु. |

Kept as the book has them: every other word; Drik Panchang does not differ from the book in any word (चंदनोघसि joined in both; नाम / नामु and तेरा / तेरो as printed).

## Readings settled
The readings the words alone leave open, each decided (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §2).

- **aarti.pad4** — दस अठा अठसठे चारे खानी इहै वरतणि है सगल संसारे: the traditional reading kept, "the eighteen Purāṇas, the sixty-eight places of pilgrimage, the four sources of birth: this is the way of the whole world". The Faridkot Teeka on this hymn (Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 694, shabad 4; our `guru-granth-sahib/commentary/pa/faridkot.json`) reads it so: the eighteen Purāṇas, the sixty-eight tīrthas and the four khāṇīs, in which the whole world's occupation lies. Meaning unchanged.
