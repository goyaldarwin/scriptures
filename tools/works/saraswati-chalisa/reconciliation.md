# Reconciliation: श्री सरस्वती चालीसा

## Witnesses
- **The book (base):** ५१ चालीसा संग्रह आरतियों सहित (Randhir Prakashan, Haridwar, first edition 2000), pp. 192–195; scriptures-data `hinduism/chalisa/books/aarti-chalisa-sangrah/` (page images i-191 … i-194), read on the page images.
- **Chalisa Mala** (a printout of indif.com; the same book folder, page images i-422 … i-424): the same version as the book, with its own slips (दुष्टजनों के पाप को in the second dohā). It prints the Sanskrit stuti "या कुन्देन्दु…" (as "बंदे इंदु सुवार हार धवला") before the dohās; the book does not, and it is not part of this text.
- **Drik Panchang** (`hinduism/chalisa/saraswati/saraswati-chalisa/printouts/drikpanchang/saraswati-mata-chalisa.html`): another version in many lines (बाल्मीकि जी थे बहम ज्ञानी, रामायण जो रचे बनाई, विनय करूं बहु भाँति घनेरी, करहु कृपा भवमुक्ति भवानी । मो कहं दास सदा निज जानी as the last chaupāī, माता सूरज कान्ति तव, अधम रामसागरहिं तुम आश्रय देउ पुनातु in the last dohā), with the same 39 chaupāīs in the same order.
- **For a doubtful reading only** (opening dohā 1, chaupāī 13): Shubh Panchang (shubhpanchang.com, the book's version: जनक जननि पद-रज, विनय करूं भाँति बहुतेरी) and AstroVed (astroved.com, Drik Panchang's version: पद कमल रज), found by web search.

The rule: keep the book; correct it only where it is clearly wrong, and only on the witnesses. The library's writing standard (docs/PLAYBOOK.md §3): a chaupāī's two halves on one line, `।` between them and `॥` at its end; a dohā's two lines, each ending `।` and `॥`; the book's commas are not kept.

|---|---|---|---|
| opening dohā 1 | जनक जननि पदम दुरज | जनक जननि पद कमल रज | दुरज is not a word, and the quarter is short of its 13 mātrās. The witnesses all mean "the dust of the (lotus) feet" but differ in form: Chalisa Mala पद्मराज, Shubh Panchang पद-रज (both still short), Drik Panchang and AstroVed पद कमल रज, the only form that is whole in sense and metre. Settled under Readings settled. |
| chaupāī 3 | रूप चर्तुभुजधारी माता | रूप चतुर्भुजधारी माता | चर्तुभुज is a misprint of चतुर्भुज ("four-armed"), as Chalisa Mala and Drik Panchang have it. |
| chaupāī 6 | तब प्रसाद जानै संसारा | तव प्रसाद जानै संसारा | The sense is "by your grace" (तव प्रसाद), as Chalisa Mala and Drik Panchang have it; तब ("then") leaves the line without its subject. |
| chaupāī 13 | विनय करु भाँति बहुतेरी | विनय करउं भाँति बहुतेरी | The speaker pleads ("I plead"), and the half-line needs 16 mātrās: करु (an imperative, 14 mātrās) is a slip. Chalisa Mala has करउं, which fills the metre; Shubh Panchang करूं. |
| chaupāī 26 | विष्णु रुद्र अज सकहिन मारी | विष्णु रुद्र अज सकहिं न मारी | सकहिन is सकहिं न run together without its anusvāra ("cannot kill"), as Drik Panchang and Shubh Panchang have it. |
| chaupāī 36 | धूपादिक नवैद्य चढ़ावै | धूपादिक नैवेद्य चढ़ावै | नवैद्य is a misprint of नैवेद्य ("food offering"), as Chalisa Mala and Drik Panchang have it. |

Kept as the book has them, against Drik Panchang: बन्दौं, अविनाशी, तबही धर्म की फीकी ज्योति (Drik जबहि … ज्योती), तबहि मातु का निज अवतारा (Drik ले), बाल्मीकि जी थे हत्यारा, रामचरित जो रचे बनाई । आदि कवि पदवी को पाई, विद्वाना (Drik विद्धाना), तिन्ह (Drik तिन्हहिं), सोई, करई, जननि, करऊ, विष्णु से (Drik विष्णू ते), नहीं मोरा, मातु सहाय कीन्ह … बुद्धि विपरीत भई (Drik भई … करी), छण महु संहारेउ तेहिमाता, सुरमुनि, काँपी, बिनऊं, जगप्रसिद्ध … शुंभनिशुंभा, छण में वधे (Drik छिन में बधे), बुद्धि फेरेऊ, एहिविधि … तू कीन्हा … सबको, रुद्र (Drik रूद्र), दुर्ग आदि हरनी, नृप कोपित को (Drik जो), पोत के भंजे (Drik भंगे), सबै छाँडि, ईशा, करैं हमेशा, सत बारा (Drik शत), रामसागर बाधि हेतु भवानी । कीजै कृपा दास निज जानी, मातु सूर्य कान्ति तव, डूबन से … परूँ न मैं भव कूप, बल बुद्धि विद्या देहु मोहि सुनहु सरस्वती मातु । राम सागर अधम को आश्रय तू ही ददातु (Chalisa Mala the same).

## Readings settled
- chalisa.do1 — the book's पदम दुरज: the text's पद कमल रज stands (the table above): the only witnessed form whole in sense and metre (Drik Panchang, AstroVed).
- chalisa.cau29 — दुर्ग आदि हरनी: read as "slayer of the demon Durga (Durgama) and the others". cau27–28 follow Devī Māhātmya 11 (Raktadantikā, Śatākṣī), where the Goddess slays the great asura Durgama and so is named Durgā (11.45–46: दुर्गमाख्यं महासुरम् … दुर्गा देवीति विख्यातं); cau28 names her Durgā for that deed, and Monier-Williams gives Durga as the Asura slain by Durgā (SkandaP.). The meaning changes from "remover of hardships".
