# Brief for writing our own Punjabi and English meanings of Sri Guru Granth Sahib

You are writing the Punjabi and English meanings, line by line, of a batch of shabads (or pauris, saloks, stanzas) of Sri Guru Granth Sahib, for a free, ad-free scripture-reading app owned by Darwin Goyal. His words: **accuracy and understanding the context are what make a good meaning; take reference from all the materials we have, as you need them.** Accuracy comes first, then unambiguity, clarity and consistency; none of them is ever traded for distance from another translation. No Hindi.

**Before anything else:** `python3 tools/meanings/sanskrit_dict.py fetch && python3 tools/meanings/sanskrit_dict.py check` and `python3 tools/meanings/teeka.py fetch <scriptures-data checkout>` (the session names the checkout). If either fails, stop and report it.

## Read first (binding)
1. `tools/meanings/style.md`: §1, §2, **§12 (Gurbani)**, **§13 (Punjabi)**, §5 (English), §9.
2. The voice to match, owner-approved: our Japji Sahib and Sukhmani Sahib meanings, `tools/works/japji-sahib/meanings/{pa,en}/scriptures.json` and `tools/works/sukhmani-sahib/meanings/{pa,en}/scriptures.json` (read several items in each), and the Granth's meanings already written (`tools/works/guru-granth-sahib/meanings/{pa,en}/scriptures.json`, if present): render a heading, a refrain or a term as they already do.
3. Your batch file `batch-NN.md`: the verses just before (context), then each verse with its id, type, writer and Ang, its lines numbered from 0 with their transliteration, and our Punjabi word meanings where we have them (often few: they are aids, not authority).

## The materials (use whatever a line needs)
- **Prof. Sahib Singh's Darpan** (©; the authority on Gurbani grammar) and the **Faridkot Teeka** (public domain, old idiom): `python3 tools/meanings/teeka.py look <Gurmukhi word> --lines 4` finds every place a word is in either, with its ਪਦਅਰਥ word glosses and grammar notes; `grep -n "<a few words of the line>" ~/.cache/scriptures-dict/darpan.txt` finds the shabad (the Darpan's quoted Gurbani may show a sihari on the letter before; search on two or three words).
- **Bhai Vir Singh's Santhya** (Ang 1 to about 607; an exposition with context, the allusions and the traditional readings): its OCR is in the scriptures-data checkout, `sikhism/gurbani/guru-granth-sahib/books/santhya/ocr/pothi-N.txt`; `grep -n` a few words of the line (about 95% correct; never trust one OCR word against the Gurmukhi).
- **The Shabdarth** (SGPC 1936–41: word meanings and short explanations of the whole Granth): `sikhism/gurbani/guru-granth-sahib/books/shabdarth/ocr/`, the same way.
- **`batch-NN.refs.md`**: for every line, the line-by-line translations of the Shabad OS database (Sahib Singh, Manmohan Singh, Faridkot in Punjabi; Sant Singh Khalsa, Manmohan Singh in English). Most are copyright.
- **Dictionaries:** Sanskrit-derived words in Monier-Williams and Apte (`python3 tools/meanings/sanskrit_dict.py look <Devanagari word>`), Braj and Persian-derived words in Platts (`python3 tools/meanings/platts.py look <word>`, after `platts.py fetch` from the checkout), our `library/glossary/pa.json`. The Mahan Kosh's text cannot be extracted; its senses come through the Darpan's and the Shabdarth's glosses.

## How to write each verse
1. **Understand the whole verse first, in its context.** Read the whole shabad before any line: its **Rahao** (ਰਹਾਉ) line is its central thought, and every stanza unfolds it; the heading names the raag and the writer (ਮਹਲਾ ੧ = Guru Nanak Dev Ji, ਮਹਲਾ ੩ = Guru Amar Das Ji …; a Bhagat by name). Know who speaks and to whom: the Guru to God, to the mind (ਮਨ), to the seeker or a Sikh, to a pandit, a yogi or a qazi; the soul-bride (ਸੋਹਾਗਣਿ, ਕਾਮਣਿ, ਧਨ) longing for the Husband Lord (ਸਹੁ, ਕੰਤ, ਪਿਰੁ); a vaar's salok and its pauri. Read the verses just before it (a vaar, a run of ashtapadis, a chhant follows on).
2. **Then the words and their grammar.** Gurbani's endings decide the sense: aunkar ੁ on a masculine noun marks it singular (subject or object); sihari ਿ a locative, instrumental, a conjunct participle or a feminine; a verb's person, number and mood (ਕਰੇ, ਕਰਹਿ, ਕਰਿ; a command stays a command, a statement a statement); a negation kept. Look up every word you are not certain of (the materials above), the Darpan's ਪਦਅਰਥ for its grammar.
3. **Draft the Punjabi and the English, one reading,** from the Gurmukhi (the Punjabi is never translated from your English). Then read the commentaries and the refs line by line to check your reading. Where a commentary reads a line otherwise, take the reading the grammar and the context bear out (the sense belongs to no one; their wording does), and record a genuine difference in your doubts file with your decision. A doubt is decided by you, never left in the meaning.
4. **Notes, few** (the owner: lines and few notes): only where a reader needs one to understand the line: a term whose Gurbani sense is not the everyday one (ਹੁਕਮੁ, ਸਬਦੁ, ਹਉਮੈ, ਸਹਜੁ, ਤੁਰੀਆ), an allusion (Dhru, Prahlad, Ajamal, the 68 tirathas, Dharam Rai and Chitr-Gupt, a story of the Puranas), or a grammar point that decides the sense. One or two short sentences each, Punjabi and English saying the same. No sermon, no summary of what the lines already say, no conclusion the verse does not draw, no history of the shabad. A term explained once in the part is not explained again.

## Rules (style.md §12, §13, and these)
- **One piece per verse line,** in order, each a sentence or clause under its line (a sentence may run on to the next line's piece). Punjabi and English cut at the same lines.
- **Headings:** the raag-and-writer line gets its piece: "ਰਾਗ ਆਸਾ ਵਿੱਚ ਪਹਿਲੇ ਗੁਰੂ (ਗੁਰੂ ਨਾਨਕ ਦੇਵ ਜੀ) ਦੀ ਬਾਣੀ।" / "Raag Asa, by the First Guru (Guru Nanak Dev Ji)." (a Bhagat: "Raag Gauri, the hymn of Bhagat Kabir Ji."; ਘਰੁ ੨ kept after the raag: "Raag Gauri, Ghar 2, by …"; Sri Raag is "Sri Raag" (ਸਿਰੀਰਾਗ in Punjabi, one word as the Gurmukhi spells it), never "Raag Sri Raag"; the raag is named as the app names its part (Raag Asa, Raag Gauri, Raag Majh: `library/sikhism/gurbani/guru-granth-sahib/work.json` → `parts`); ਪਹਰੇ, ਛੰਤ, ਅਸਟਪਦੀਆ, ਵਾਰ named as the line names them). ੴ ਸਤਿਗੁਰ ਪ੍ਰਸਾਦਿ ॥ is always "ਪਰਮਾਤਮਾ ਇੱਕ ਹੈ, ਸਭ ਵਿੱਚ ਵਿਆਪਕ ਕਰਤਾ; ਉਹ ਸਤਿਗੁਰੂ ਦੀ ਕਿਰਪਾ ਨਾਲ ਮਿਲਦਾ ਹੈ।" / "Ik Oankar: God is One, the all-pervading Creator, realised through the True Guru's grace." (the Sukhmani's). The full Mool Mantar renders as in the Japji. A heading that names a bani puts its title first, as the line does ("Sohila: Raag Gauri Deepaki, by the First Guru (Guru Nanak Dev Ji)."). A bare ਸਲੋਕੁ ॥, ਪਉੜੀ ॥, ਅਸਟਪਦੀ ॥, ਛੰਤੁ ॥ line gets "".
- **ਰਹਾਉ** (Pause): the line's meaning only; the word itself is not rendered (the reader sees it in the verse). No verse or stanza numbers anywhere: the exporter adds them.
- **ਨਾਨਕ** in a line: §12's rule, the same in both languages. The Gurus' signature is "Nanak"; a Bhagat's is his name (ਕਹੁ ਕਬੀਰ "Says Kabir").
- **God and the Guru:** §12 (English) and §13 (Punjabi). Pronouns for God lower case in English; God addressed ਤੂੰ in Punjabi, "you" in English.
- **Settled renderings in the Granth:** ਗੁਰਮੁਖਿ as an adverb, "through the Guru" / "ਗੁਰੂ ਰਾਹੀਂ" (Gurmukh, the person, stays Gurmukh / ਗੁਰਮੁਖ); ਹਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਨਾਮੁ "the Name of the Lord, Har Har" / "ਹਰੀ ਦਾ ਨਾਮ"; ਧ੍ਰਿਗੁ "cursed" / "ਧ੍ਰਿਗ"; ਨਾਸ਼ਵੰਤ with ਸ਼. A name follows its Gurmukhi spelling: ਗੋਬਿੰਦ Gobind, ਗੋਵਿੰਦ Govind. ਧਰਮ ਰਾਇ / ਧਰਮ ਰਾਜਾ: Dharam Raj / ਧਰਮ ਰਾਜ (as the Japji). Vaar headings: "Sri Raag ki Vaar: by the Fourth Guru (Guru Ram Das Ji), with saloks." / "ਸਿਰੀਰਾਗ ਕੀ ਵਾਰ: ਚੌਥੇ ਗੁਰੂ (ਗੁਰੂ ਰਾਮਦਾਸ ਜੀ) ਦੀ ਬਾਣੀ, ਸਲੋਕਾਂ ਸਮੇਤ।"; ਸਲੋਕ ਮਃ ੩ "Salok, by the Third Guru (Guru Amar Das Ji)." / "ਤੀਜੇ ਗੁਰੂ (ਗੁਰੂ ਅਮਰਦਾਸ ਜੀ) ਦਾ ਸਲੋਕ।"; ਮਃ ੩ alone "By the Third Guru (Guru Amar Das Ji)." / "ਤੀਜੇ ਗੁਰੂ (ਗੁਰੂ ਅਮਰਦਾਸ ਜੀ) ਦੀ ਬਾਣੀ।"; ਪਉੜੀ "". Ashtapadis: "Sri Raag, Ghar 1, Ashtapadis, by the First Guru (Guru Nanak Dev Ji)." / "ਸਿਰੀਰਾਗ, ਘਰ ੧ ਵਿੱਚ ਪਹਿਲੇ ਗੁਰੂ (ਗੁਰੂ ਨਾਨਕ ਦੇਵ ਜੀ) ਦੀਆਂ ਅਸਟਪਦੀਆਂ।" (the raag as the part names it). Raag Gauri's forms: Gauri Guareri, Gauri Cheti, Gauri Bairagan, Gauri Poorbi (ਪੂਰਬੀ), Gauri Dakhani, Gauri Majh, Gauri Mala. A shabad the Granth repeats (a Sohila shabad in Gauri, say) takes the meanings already written for it, only its heading its own.
- **Repeated lines and refrains** are rendered the same way every time, in this batch and as the Granth's meanings already render them; list them in your refrains file.
- No quotation marks, no square brackets; round brackets only for implied words or a first gloss, sparingly. Gurmukhi Unicode NFC; Punjabi sentences end with ।, English with a full stop.
- Do **not** edit any file in the repository: write only in the batch folder.

## The copy rule
Your wording is your own. Check it: `python3 tools/meanings/overlap.py OUT/batch-NN.en.json OUT/copied-en-sant-singh-khalsa.json --report`, and the same against `copied-en-manmohan-singh.json`; the Punjabi against `copied-pa-sahib-singh.json`, `copied-pa-manmohan-singh.json`, `copied-pa-faridkot.json`. Each must end with "0 with a shared run of 10+ words". A shorter run that is the natural words, a name or the line's own order is fine; reword only what plainly follows their phrasing, changing **only the wording**.

## Output (in the batch folder; NN = your batch number)
- `batch-NN.pa.json` and `batch-NN.en.json`: `{"<id>": ["<piece for line 0>", …]}` — exactly the ids of `batch-NN.ids`, in order; each list exactly as long as the verse's lines. UTF-8, `ensure_ascii=False`.
- `batch-NN.notes.json` (may be `{}`): `{"<id>": {"pa": ["…"], "en": ["…"]}}`.
- `batch-NN.refrains.json`: `{"<Gurmukhi line or phrase>": {"pa": …, "en": …}}` (may be `{}`).
- `batch-NN.doubts.jsonl` (may be empty): `{"id", "line", "issue", "our reading", "alternatives", "decided because"}`.
- `batch-NN.words.jsonl` (may be empty): `{"id", "word", "current", "suggested": {"pa", "en"}, "reason"}` — a word meaning of `pa.json` that is wrong or lacks this line's sense.

Before finishing, verify with `python3 -c`: both files load, exact ids in order, piece counts equal to the verses' lines (`library/sikhism/gurbani/guru-granth-sahib/work.json` → `items[].v`), "" at the same lines in both, NFC, no "॥" followed by a digit; and the overlap checks pass. Final reply (short): verses and lines written, notes, overlap results (longest run per comparison), doubts, word issues.
