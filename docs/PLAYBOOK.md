# Playbook: the owner's preferences and the project's rules

Every rule here is current. When the owner states a new preference, or a lesson changes how we work, change the rule here in the same commit as the change. Do not add dates, "was / used to / superseded" notes or stories: git keeps the history. Where a rule has a reason, the reason is given in the present tense.

Contents: 1 Working with the owner · 2 Sources and accuracy · 3 Texts · 4 Meanings · 5 Word meanings · 6 The library · 7 The app · 8 Audio · 9 Apps and store · 10 Engineering · 11 Extraction techniques

**The principles, and what holds each one.** Every rule below follows from these; a rule a check can hold is held by one, so a new work cannot break it unnoticed.

| Principle | Held by |
|---|---|
| One source of truth for each thing: a verse's text (its work's `text.txt` or generator), its number (`work.json` → `cite`), a word's sense (the language's dictionary, a work's own sense only where it differs), a time (the recording's inputs) | `regen_check.py` (generators and exporters remake every file), `tools/text/cite.py`, `export.py` (a work sense that repeats the shared one is refused) |
| Every work the same: one folder format, one exporter, the same structure (part › section › verse, written down in its README), the same experience in the app | `library.py` (folders, READMEs' Structure, types named alike, a plan's own word for its days, copied meanings named alike), `export.py`, `ux_test.py` (every check run on every work), `docs/WORKS.md` (made by the build from the library: every work side by side, and what is not yet the same) |
| One word for one thing, a setting only where it does something | `build.py` (the app's English labels), `library.py` (`lines`, `notes`: Meaning per line and Footnotes show only where a work has them) |
| One standard for writing (NFC, ॥, Pāṇini's Sanskrit, ISO 15919) | `tools/text/standard.py`, applied by every export and checked by the build |
| Ids are permanent | `history_check.py` |
| Fix, don't annotate; record every choice | the work's `reconciliation.md` (reviewed by hand) |
| Everything that broke gets a test | `ux_test.py`, `smoke_test.py`, the self-tests of `standard.py` and `cite.py` |

## 1. Working with the owner
- **Accuracy over speed.** Then unambiguity, clarity, consistency and structure. The owner "loves and admires consistency".
- **Plain words.** Say what changed, what to check, and one next step. The owner reads on his phone.
- **Never leave him looking at "processing":** send a short progress line (what is happening, what is done, roughly how long the rest takes).
- **Ask only when a decision is really his.** Otherwise pick the sensible option and say so.
- **Show it working:** test at phone size (390×844) and look at the screenshots before saying it is done.
- **Be honest about limits:** what the session cannot reach (most websites, Hugging Face, YouTube), what runs on the workspace (speech recognition does not use Claude usage; Claude's own reading and writing does), and that background jobs stop after 2 hours.
- **The owner shares files himself** (book PDFs, printouts, screenshots, a recording's audio file with the YouTube id at the end of its name). Nobody else is ever asked to upload anything.
- Only the owner's repositories for this project are read or changed (`goyaldarwin/scriptures`, `goyaldarwin/scriptures-data`).

## 2. Sources and accuracy
- **Never write a scripture text from memory.** Each work's text comes from the source the owner gave for it (a book, a printout, a video's on-screen text, a web page he allows), named in the work's README.
- **Fix, don't annotate.** A discrepancy is fixed in the data, never shown in the app (no "पाठभेद" tags, no variant markers, no notes about accuracy). Every fix is recorded in the work's `reconciliation.md`: what the source has, what the text has, why.
- **Correct a source only where it is clearly wrong:** a typing slip, a broken word, a missing space, a sense that does not fit, broken Sanskrit. Otherwise keep its words.
- **Decide, never leave doubts for the owner** (the owner: "Don't create a backlog of doubts for me… search online trusted sources and decide yourself"). Settle every reading from the witnesses, grammar, metre, Monier-Williams and Apte and the traditional commentaries, and record the decision and its reason in `reconciliation.md`; where nothing settles it, the base text stays as printed.
- **Witnesses:** the one the owner prefers is the base, the others check it. Agreement of witnesses that share one edition is not by itself proof the base is wrong: the base is corrected only where its reading is clearly an error (a non-word, a broken form, broken metre, a dropped sign) and the witnesses give the right form; a valid reading of the base edition stays. Each witness's text is kept in the work's `source/witness/`, and every reviewed difference with its decision in `source/`.
- **Widely available texts** (a Chālīsā, an āratī) may come from two or three good web sources when the owner says so; reconcile word by word.
- **A doubt in a text may be settled by a web search** (the owner: "look up trusted or online sources yourself to resolve confusions"): only for a line the witnesses leave in doubt, never to make a text; sites that copy one another count as one witness (Bhakti Bharat prints Drik Panchang's text), and sites with editorial standing come first (Gita Press, Bhāratkosh, sanskritdocuments.org). The base can lose: a reading the base and its copy share is corrected when independent sources show it is a corruption (the Gaṇeśa Chālīsā's सम्बन्ध for सम्वत्). Every source used is named in the work's `reconciliation.md`.
- **The Chālīsā and Āratī shelves:** the printed ५१ चालीसा संग्रह आरतियों सहित (Randhir Prakashan, in scriptures-data with its OCR) is the base where it has the text; Drik Panchang's pages (a typed copy of the same collection) are the base where it does not, and a witness everywhere.
- **Spelling in Tulasīdāsa's works** follows the Gita Press house style of our Mānas (ए not ये; ब/स forms; joined compounds; no hyphens); where sources disagree on a word, the metre decides.
- **A verse that belongs to a bigger work is taken from it by id, never retyped** (`[manas bal.17.so17]` in a `text.txt`), so a correction there reaches every copy, and a check proves the copy is equal.
- **Never route around a blocked site or a copyright limit.** From these sessions the shell reaches GitHub and little else; the fetch tool returns summaries, not whole pages. Ask the owner for the page as a PDF (into `scriptures-data`), or to allow the site.
- **`goyaldarwin/scriptures-data` is private, always.** Its raw files are never copied here; only what the build makes from them.
- **Reference books** (dictionaries, commentaries) are used to check facts, never copied (`tools/meanings/style.md` §11).

## 3. Texts
- **The verse and its number are the core of each work.** Item ids are permanent: bookmarks, notes, progress and audio times point at them. A removed verse's id is never reused; a renamed one goes in `work.json` → `renamed`.
- **Change a text in its generator** (`text.txt`, `settle.py`, the exporter), never only in a generated file: a regeneration undoes it. Before committing a regenerated file, diff it against the previous one and explain every change.
- **Removing a verse:** drop it in the generator, re-point any topic that started at it, regenerate meanings and audio.
- **Verses put before a work's text** (an opening a recording sings) need `work.json` → `prepended`, so older saved places move forward once.
- **One caupāī per item** in the Mānas: where Gita Press prints two or three as one, they are separate items; the joint meaning sits under the last part.
- **Pages follow the book's own sections, never topics.** A topic starting mid-section shows its heading inside the page, before its verse. Chaupāīs belong to the dohā that follows them ("Dohā 2" = the chaupāīs before dohā 2 and dohā 2).
- **Opening verses are named by type and number** ("Śloka 1–7", "Sorathā 5"), never "Dohā 0" or "Invocation".
- **A work declares its own verse types** in `work.json` → `types` (name and short tag); the app knows the Mānas's (चौपाई, दोहा, सोरठा, छंद, श्लोक).
- **One number per verse, from one rule** (the owner: one source of truth; the reader, the player and search never take a verse's number from different places). Every work is part › section › verse; a verse's `n` is its number at its own level, as the book prints it (the Kaṭha's 1.1.1 ends ॥१॥: n is 1). How the work cites a verse is declared once, in `work.json` → `cite` (`tools/text/cite.py`): the Mānas by dohā (Verse 106.3 is the third verse under Doha 106; a dohā by its own number), the Kaṭha adhyāya.vallī.mantra (Mantra 2.1.1), the Śrī Rudram anuvāka.mantra (Mantra 11.17), the Sukhmani Sahib ashtapadi.pauri (Pauri 1.8), the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa sarga.verse (Shloka 15.30), Sri Guru Granth Sahib Ang.place (Shabad 14.2: the Ang a verse begins on and its place among the verses beginning there; the end marks ॥੪॥੧॥੩੪॥ count stanzas, groups and raags and restart, `cite.mark` "none"), every other work the verse's own number. The exporter writes the citation into every verse (`c`), and the reader's places, the player, Resume, Go to / Back to and the number search all read it; the app never makes a number of its own. A verse taken from another work keeps that work's citation. The build refuses a number printed at a verse's end that is not its own, and two verses of one type cited alike in one part (a named section counts as part of the place); where the book itself numbers a run again (a second run of chhands under one dohā), `cite.repeat` names it.
- **Sections that restart the numbering have names** (`work.json` → `sections`): the Dhūpa Āratī's Namaskārāṣṭaka is "Namaskarashtak · Stanza 1", never a second "Stanza 1"; the Mānas's closing ślokas are "Closing · Shloka 1".
- **How verses are written: one standard for every work**, applied by `tools/text/standard.py` when an exporter writes (`jsonio.dump_library`), and checked by the build (a verse or transliteration that differs fails it). A source file keeps its printed form; the published text follows the standard:
  - **Unicode NFC, everywhere** (verses, meanings, headings): ड़ is ड + ़ as a keyboard types it, never the single letter U+095C, so search and word look-up see one spelling; the build refuses a work file that is not NFC.
  - **॥ is one character** (U+0965), never two daṇḍas, in verses and meanings alike. **Verse numbers are compact** (`॥१॥`, `॥१३अ॥`, `॥३ (क)॥`: no space before the closing ॥, and a dohā's letter in brackets as Gita Press prints it) so they never wrap away from the line; the texts keep Devanagari digits, and the app shows every number as 1 2 3 (below). Marks like आ॰ / ध्रु॰ use the abbreviation sign ॰ (U+0970), never a zero. **An āratī's verse has no comma** (its printed sources' commas are not kept; a pause within a line is a space; `library.py` checks it); the Vedic and Sanskrit prose that prints them (the Rudram's pāṭha, the Saptaśatī's viniyogas) keeps its own.
  - **Sanskrit follows Pāṇini** (the owner): ॐ for the sacred syllable (the pluta ओ३म् stays as printed); inside a word, a nasal before a stop is written as that stop's own nasal letter (सङ्कल्प, पञ्च, दण्ड, अन्त, कम्प), never the dot; a word-final m is म् at a pause (before । or ॥) and before a vowel, ं only before a consonant; the avagraha ऽ marks an elided a (it cannot be added by rule: `avagraha_candidates` lists places to check by eye against the source).
  - **ꣳ in the Yajurveda's texts** (shelf path under `veda/yajurveda/`): the anusvāra before श ष स ह र is ꣳ, as the Yajurveda recites it; elsewhere it is ं.
  - **The transliteration is ISO 15919**, made from the verse, never typed: r̥ is ऋ and ṛ is ड़; ṁ anusvāra, m̐ candrabindu, ḥ visarga, ’ avagraha, gͫ ꣳ; Vedic accents on their vowel (◌̍ udātta, ◌̠ anudātta, ◌̎ double svarita). A daṇḍa is ",", a line ending in ॥ ends with ".", a number is ` ॥N॥`. Gurmukhi is transliterated the same way, letter for letter (each letter is its Devanagari twin; tippi and bindi ṁ, addak doubles the consonant, ੴ ik oaṅkār). An i or u vowel after an a is ï ü (गउ gaü), never to be read as ai, au.
  - **Words are looked up by their spelling folded** (`fold`: class nasal, ꣳ and final म् as ं), so the dictionaries need one entry for गङ्गा and गंगा; the app's `fold()` is the same rule (the smoke test compares them).
- **Every verse has a language** (`l` on the item, else the work's `lang`), and every language is in `library/glossary/languages.json`. A text in a new language brings its own language and dictionary (Braj: `bra`), never a near language's.
- **Vedic accents** (svaras) are kept in the text; the app strips them only when looking a word up.
- **Each āratī is its own work**, on its deity's shelf (`hinduism/aarti/<deity>/<work>`), even when several come from one page. **The refrain (टेक) is shown once**; the "॥ ॐ जय शिव… ॥" cues after each stanza and the closing repeat are left out, unless the recording sings a different closing refrain (the Śiva Āratī's `aarti.tek2`).
- **A popular part of a big work can be a work of its own** (the Sundarakāṇḍa beside the Mānas): it is generated from the big work and never written into it; its own additions (opening prayers, Chālīsā, āratī) come from its own `text.txt`.
- **An āratī's text follows the version sung in its chosen recording** (the owner: "re-order the verses to suit the recording"; the Śiva and Gaṇeśa āratīs): where it sings the verses in another order, or in another of the written versions, the text takes the recording's order and wording, each word as a written witness spells it, and `reconciliation.md` says so. A verse of the source that the recording leaves out stays in the text, untimed.
- **A recording's opening that is not in the book is added as text too, and timed**, so the reader sees what is being sung (the Sundarakāṇḍa's opening prayers).
- **The closing verse is "समापन (पुष्पिका)" / "Closing words (iti)"**, not "colophon".

## 4. Meanings
- **Our own meanings, everywhere.** Where a meaning source is someone else's copyright, we write our own Hindi and English (and Punjabi for Gurbani) **from the original verse, never by rewording a translation**. They are sources `hi-scriptures`, `en-scriptures`, `pa-scriptures`, titled "भावार्थ — शास्त्र" / "Hindi meaning — Scriptures" (and the matching English and Punjabi titles); the app never mentions AI.
- **Which source a reader sees first** is the source's `order`. Ours is first in every work except the Rāmacaritamānasa, where Gita Press stays first until the owner says otherwise. Other sources stay selectable under ☰ › More settings › Sources.
- **For Sanskrit texts (the Upaniṣads, the Vedic hymns), our meanings are concise and grounded in tradition:** one to three sentences per verse, following the traditional reading (Śaṅkara for the Upaniṣads, Sāyaṇa for Vedic hymns), other readings briefly in a footnote. (The Mānas, its Sanskrit ślokas included, gives one reading with no footnotes; the readings it takes are in its `reconciliation.md`.) The owner: "Use your own intelligence… Don't copy word for word… Bring the context of Hinduism."
- **Gurbani meanings are elaborate as well as accurate** (the owner, for the Japji Sahib): the line-by-line meaning stays exact, what the line says and nothing more, and the elaboration goes into notes in all three languages: a term (Hukam, Sachiar, Kudrat), an allusion (the bull Dhaul, the 68 tirathas, the four khanis), the grammar that decides a reading, a traditional second reading. Each is written from the Gurmukhi and checked against Prof. Sahib Singh's Darpan, the Faridkot Teeka and the published translations, never copied.
- **The whole Guru Granth Sahib has Punjabi meanings only** (the owner): copied public-domain sources (the Shabdarth, the Santhya, the Faridkot Teeka), no Hindi or English. Bhai Vir Singh's Santhya is the default meaning where it exists (Ang 1 to about 607), the Shabdarth elsewhere and the second meaning there (the owner); the plan is `docs/ROADMAP.md`, item 0.
- **Line by line, when the reader chooses it.** Our meanings are stored whole and one piece per verse line (`p`); a line whose sense cannot stand alone gives its piece to the next line. By default every meaning is shown whole after the verse (the owner prefers it); the switch "Meaning per line" ("Split the meanings by line") under ☰ › More settings, shown only in a work with a meaning given line by line (as Footnotes and Commentary only where there are some: a setting is never shown where it does nothing), shows each of our pieces under its line ("whatever our line in the verse, its meaning below it wherever present"). Sources without pieces always show the whole meaning after the verse. Each line sits on its own meaning as one pair, with no ॰ between them; a short faint saffron dash parts one pair from the next, so the full-width rule under the verse is the only long line and clearly ends it, as in a verse with its meaning whole.
- **The verse number ends the meaning**, added by the exporter from the verse (`tools/meanings/versenum.py`), never typed into a meaning file.
- **Copying is checked:** no run of 10 or more words shared with a copyrighted meaning; runs of 6–9 are read (`tools/meanings/overlap.py`). A same-language meaning (our Punjabi beside a Punjabi commentary) also gets a side-by-side copy audit, because the checker misses a sentence-by-sentence paraphrase.
- **A doubt of reading** is settled by the session (§2: decide, never leave doubts for the owner) and recorded with its reason; the meaning gives the plain reading. The existing files in `docs/reviews/` are settled the same way. Before following Gita Press on a doubt, check Monier-Williams or Apte, the grammar, and an independent translation (Growse, when the owner provides a copy; none is in scriptures-data yet): most web meanings copy Gita Press.
- **A publisher's meaning that we show is kept as printed:** only extraction breaks and plain typos are fixed.
- **Word meanings from witnesses' word-by-word glosses are copied as they give them** (the owner: "we should use as it is… just compare two text if needed"). In the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa a word's English is IIT Kanpur's gloss, else valmikiramayan.net's; where both gloss it and differ, the one that fits the verse. Each gloss is taken whole, never cut down or reworded. A word or part neither glosses (or glosses wrongly for the verse) is written by us. Every entry's origin is recorded (`source/words/src.json`: iitk, vr, ours, or several joined with +). The Hindi, root, grammar and parts are always ours, checked in Monier-Williams and Apte.
- **The Śrī Śivamahāpurāṇa's English is the owner's drafts** (written from each verse with Claude), brought to the style sheet and the speakers named from one table, without the writer–reviewer–auditor steps (the owner: "fix the style and let's use these meanings till we have something better"); a reviewed meaning replaces them verse by verse.
- **The Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā's Hindi and English are the owner's drafts** (written from each verse with Claude, in the JSON he gave with the text), brought to the style sheet by rules in one script (`tools/works/bhagavad-gita/source/meanings.py`) and hand fixes with their reasons (`meaning_fixes.json`), without the writer–reviewer–auditor steps, as the Śrī Śivamahāpurāṇa's; a reviewed meaning replaces them verse by verse. Public-domain translations copied beside ours (Besant, Telang) are copied sources like any other: ours shares no run of ten words with them.
- **Style** (names, epithets, recurring terms, honorifics, Hindi joined postpositions, English with IAST names): `tools/meanings/style.md`.
- **Headings (topics):** our own wording where a work shows our meanings by default (the Sundarakāṇḍa: `tools/works/manas/topics.json`); the Mānas keeps Gita Press's while Gita Press is its default. A heading or meaning the owner has already approved (the Sundarakāṇḍa and its Kiṣkindhā lead-in) is changed only to fix an error or a copy; a new batch never rewrites it for taste.
- **Accuracy and consistency first, never traded away.** The owner: "We cannot forego consistency or accuracy." No meaning is made less accurate, less consistent or less natural to keep it away from a copyrighted translation.
- **The sense belongs to no one; only the wording is ours to make.** Where Gita Press reads a verse rightly, ours says the same thing in our own words (the owner: "I don't like changing them or distorting the meanings to match the copyright meanings"). When wording must change to avoid a copy, only the wording changes, and the new piece is read against the verse again.
- **From the verse in its context:** the meaning comes from the verse, its word meanings, the dictionaries and its context in the work overall and in the nearby verses (who speaks to whom, what has just happened, what the moment means in the work), the whole draft written before any published meaning is opened (`tools/meanings/style.md` §1).
- **Aphorisms and lessons are presented well:** a clear, complete sentence that reads on its own, in the verse's image, with no moral added (the owner: "Some of the lines contain very good aphorisms and lessons for the society. We should present them well").
- **Lines about a group of people are fair, never distorted:** respectful words, the milder sense where the word has one (ताड़ना → शिक्षा in sundar.59.cau3, the owner's choice), never harsher than the verse, the speaker made clear; what the words plainly say is not softened (aranya.34.cau1, the owner's choice). `style.md` §2.
- **Dictionaries first:** Monier-Williams and Apte are downloaded before any meaning is written, reviewed or applied (`python3 tools/meanings/sanskrit_dict.py fetch`, then `check`). They live outside the repository, so every new machine or cloud session starts without them: run it at the start of every meaning session. Writers and reviewers look up every hard Sanskrit or Sanskrit-derived word in them, beside our glossary; `prepare.py` and `apply_batches.py` refuse to run without them, and `look` downloads them when missing.
- **The Mānas, kāṇḍa by kāṇḍa** (`docs/HOW-TO.md`, "Write our own meanings for a kāṇḍa of the Mānas"; the briefs in `tools/meanings/briefs/`): batches of about 50 verses (`prepare.py`) go to a writer, then an independent reviewer, then a separate copy auditor who reads every line beside Gita Press and rewords any sentence that follows theirs with words swapped, keeping its sense exactly, then an accuracy and dictionary checker who reads every reworded piece against the verse again (a rewrite made to avoid a copy can drift) and checks the batch's hard words; each later fix wins. Then `apply_batches.py` (it refuses a batch that missed a step), a consistency pass over the kāṇḍa (names, speech openings, refrains, headings) and the word issues into the dictionary; one branch and pull request per kāṇḍa; doubts in `docs/reviews/manas-<kāṇḍa>-doubts.jsonl`.

## 5. Word meanings
- **They are for learning:** "these are the tiny steps someone is taking to learn Sanskrit… we cannot afford mistakes." Every word of every verse has a meaning; meaning, root (`r`), grammar (`g`) and parts (`p`) agree. The one exception is a work the owner publishes before its word meanings are written (`work.json` → `"words": "partial"`, the Śrīmad Bhāgavata): a tapped word without an entry says its meaning is not added yet.
- **The Śrī Śivamahāpurāṇa's word meanings are the owner's word-by-word English glosses** (the owner: "since we already have word by word meanings, let's use them"), lined up with the words as printed and gathered per word (`tools/works/shiva-purana/source/words.py`): English only, in the work's own dictionary, until the Hindi, root and grammar are written (`"words": "partial"`).
- **The Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā's word meanings are the owner's word-by-word glosses** (Hindi and English), lined up with the words as printed and gathered per word (`tools/works/bhagavad-gita/source/words.py`), in the work's own dictionary; a word the shared dictionary has is left to it. The owner's copy of *Bhagavad-gītā As It Is* (Hindi, © BBT) checks them and is never copied. Root and grammar are still to be written.
- **One dictionary per language** (`library/glossary/<lang>.json`), never merged: the same spelling means different things in different languages (या, परे, ते, करि, हर). Lookups follow the language's fallback chain.
- **A word with several senses lists them all** ("जाओ / जिसे, जिसके"); the verse's meaning uses only the one its context needs. A sense that fits only one work goes in that work's own `glossary.json`. A big work loaded by part (the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa) keeps the senses of the words only it has in its own glossary too, one file per part, so no other work downloads them; a word the shared dictionary already has is never repeated there (the export refuses it).
- **`library/glossary/*.json` are the master copies:** exporters only add new words, never overwrite. A correction is made there directly, with the reason in the commit message.
- **Checked in trusted dictionaries** (`tools/meanings/style.md` §11): Monier-Williams and Apte for Sanskrit; Platts first for Awadhi and Braj; the Hindī Śabdasāgara; McGregor read on its page images (its OCR is unreliable); Mahan Kosh for Punjabi.
- **The hard words of our meanings can be tapped too** (the owner: the words in the meanings are sometimes hard to understand): only the hard ones, faintly underlined, so a tap never opens an empty drawer (`"hard": true`, `docs/LIBRARY.md` §5). An entry gives a simple Hindi meaning and an English one, checked like any word meaning (`tools/meanings/style.md` §11), never a dictionary's wording.
- Open questions about words are in `docs/reviews/dictionary-doubts.jsonl`.

## 6. The library
- **One website** (https://scriptures.craadle.org, published from `main`), installable on the phone; no ads, ever.
- **A shelf hierarchy, tradition first:** `<tradition>/<genre>/<group…>/<work>` (`hinduism/granth/tulsidas/ramcharitmanas`, `hinduism/stotra/rama/ramraksha`, `hinduism/veda/yajurveda/krishna-yajurveda/upanishad/katha-upanishad`, `sikhism/gurbani/guru-arjan-dev/sukhmani-sahib`). Reuse levels; think of the whole future library when adding one. A work's id is permanent; its place on the shelf may change. **The app shows no tradition** (the owner: one library, not a partition by faith): its top shelves are the genres, and the Sukhmani Sahib is among the Granthas (`"shelf": "granth"` on `sikhism/gurbani`); the tradition stays in the paths, in `library/` and in `scriptures-data`.
- **Every work gets the same experience:** its natural divisions, plans, bookmarks, notes, progress, search, audio and sources.
- **A work's home:** "Continue ›" beside "▶ Play all" on one line; then its parts (or, for a single-part work, its sections) and its plans, each with ▶. A work without plans or views shows no switch. Tapping a part opens it directly. **A work's other classifications** (`views`, the owner: "other classification of the content", never another day-wise plan): Sri Guru Granth Sahib shows Part · Writer · Ang. Writer lists the Gurus (with their Mahala), the Bhagats, the Bhatts and the others, each with how many verses in how many parts; tapping one opens a reading of only that writer's verses, in the Granth's order, each part and composition heading the verses in it, its picker (▾) listing the writer's parts and compositions, and search from it looking only in that writer's verses. Ang lists the Angs by the hundred; a hundred opens to its numbers; a number opens its part at the verse that Ang begins in. The Granth's other paaths (Akhand, Saptahik, Sehaj) have no day-breaks of the Granth's own, so it has no plans.
- **Plans** (30-day Māsa-pārāyaṇa, 9-day Navāhna): a day opens at its **start**; "Continue" is the separate way back to where you were. Before the first day and after the last, a plan lists what is read then (`before` / `after` in `work.json`): the Mānas shows "Pārāyaṇa vidhi", the days, "Śrī Rāmāyaṇajī kī Āratī" and "Kathā-visarjana". The part list does not show them. Each day's ▶ has under it how long that day plays ("4 h 58 m"), and at a day's end "▶ 4 h 35 m" sits under the button to the next day: the owner: it is the listening time, not the reading time, and it changes with the recording, so it goes with ▶, never beside the day's name. From the recording chosen, at the speed set, from the start of its first verse to the end of its last, pauses included.
- **The Pārāyaṇa vidhi** is one structured page of steps (Āvāhana → Pradhāna pūjā → Viniyoga → Ācamana and Prāṇāyāma → Karanyāsa → Aṅganyāsa → Dhyāna), each mantra on a card with its meaning; beginner guidance (what it means, what to do) is in its own boxes; every word on a vidhi page has a word meaning.
- **Text and meanings are separate:** the verse is the core; Hindi, English and Punjabi meanings, commentaries and audio are selectable sources; word meanings are fixed and shared.
- **Meanings, footnotes, commentary** (the owner): a meaning translates the verse; a commentary explains it (many works come with commentaries from different gurus). A work may have any number of commentaries, as of meanings and recordings, each its own source in its own language; the reader picks one in ☰ › More settings › Sources › Commentary, which starts Off and shows only for a work that has one, and it sits after the meanings under its name. Footnotes belong to the meaning or commentary they come with (no source of their own) and show by default; ☰ › More settings › Footnotes, shown only for a work with footnotes, hides them.
- **On the library page, each shelf shows two works**, the ones used most recently on this phone first, then "+N". **The library screen has no partitions** (the owner): one row per genre, with its works as buttons. **Every shelf page looks the same however deep its shelves go** (the owner: Āratī › Śrī Rāma like Vedas › Yajurveda): a shelf that holds shelves is a heading over them; a shelf that holds only works is one row with its works as buttons. A work that stands beside shelves (Sri Guru Granth Sahib among the authors of the Granthas) is shown like a shelf of one: its author over it, the work as its button.
- **An āratī is titled by its opening words** (the owner: the shelf heading already names the deity, so "Śrī Hanumānjī kī Āratī" says nothing new): "Āratī Kījai Hanumāna Lalā Kī", "Om Jaya Gaṅge Mātā", never "[deity] kī Āratī". The title is the first words of its first verse as our text has them, cut at a natural break; the deity goes in the description ("The āratī of Śrī Hanumān"), never in `from`. The one exception is an āratī known by a name of its own, such as the Dhūpa Āratī of Shirdi (`"named": true`). The library check refuses any other title, and a shelf title that differs from its work's.
- **Under a work's title: the author, and the parent text where there is one** (`work.json` → `by`, `from`) — never the publisher.
- **The greeting** (॥ जय श्रीराम ॥, ੴ …, `salute` in `library/index.json`) shows only when opening a work from the library or the tour takes more than 0.2 s, and then for at least 0.3 s; a quick open goes straight to the work's page. Never on start, refresh, Back, or for the work already open. A held greeting feels like lag.
- **Opening a work shows its page as soon as the work and its recording are here**; the meanings and dictionaries load behind it, all at once, and the reader and search wait for them when opened sooner. A quiet open (start, refresh, Back) keeps its cover until the whole work is here, so the library never shows in between; the blank cover shows a spinner after 0.3 s.
- **The app opens where it was left:** on the library if that was the last screen, else on the work's page, or in the reader at your place (`S.view`); a link to a work (`?w=`) opens that work.
- **Resume and Play all side by side, the same size:** Resume names the place inside it in the player's short form ("Lanka Kand · Verse 106.1"), Play all the whole recording's time. **Every ▶ on a work's page shows its time under it** (parts, sections, reading days alike). "Back to …" belongs to the reader of the work open: it is hidden on every other screen and cleared when another work opens.
- **What comes next shows how long it plays:** at the end of a part, its button to the next part (and at the end of a plan's day, to the next day) has "▶ 4 h 35 m" under it, in every work.
- **⏮ ⏭ after a single verse** (▶ beside it) play the previous or next verse alone and stop, one more with each press; within a section being played they step and playing goes on; past its end, that verse alone.
- **The player's label is only the place in the text, short enough for the narrow player:** the verse's citation with its type ("Mantra 2.1.1", "Doha 106", "Chhand 10.1"), where the work gives a type a shorter word, that word (`cite.word`: a chaupai is "Verse 106.3", Doha 106's third verse), and nothing else: the player names neither the part nor a named section. Resume, "↑ Go to …" and "↓ Back to …" use the same label, with the part and a named section beside it where there is room (Resume "Namaskarashtak · Stanza 1"; the pill's small line "Uttar Kand · Closing"). Outside the reader the line under it says "Go to verse ›" (the owner: nobody knew the label could be tapped), and a tap opens the verse playing; in the reader nothing is under it (the plan day and reciter were not useful there). The label keeps one size on every screen.
- **One text size for everything read or chosen:** A−/A+ size the verses and meanings, with their footnotes and commentary (a footnote a step below its meaning, never larger), and scale the library, the work's page, bookmarks, settings, every sheet (sections and days, words, About, the verse menu) and search results by the same proportion (0.88× to 1.32×); only the top bar and the player stay fixed so they always fit. Longer text wraps, grids reflow.
- **Settings say it in the fewest words** ("Meaning per line", "Review timing", "Publish changes", "Save", "Open", "Reset", "To check: 141 verses"): no mechanism, no service names.
- **English titles and labels in plain, common Indian English**: Shri Ramcharitmanas, Sundarkand, Hanuman Chalisa, Shri Ram Raksha Stotram, Goswami Tulsidas, Bal Kand, Om Jai Jagdish Hare, Shanti Paath: the shelf, each work's title, author, parts, verse types, headings and plans, the player and the app's own words. IAST marks (Śrī Rāma) are for the transliteration and the meanings only; marks confused readers in titles. The library check refuses a marked English title or label. Opening words quoted as a heading in transliteration (the Sukhmani Sahib's ashtapadis) keep the transliteration's marks.
- **About a work:** ⓘ sits under the title on the work's page; in the library a long press on a work opens the same sheet (no ⓘ on the chips: the owner found it cluttered them); it opens a sheet with the work's introduction (`intro` in `library/index.json`, a few paragraphs of prose; else the shelf's one line), and on the work's page language, length, listening time with reciter, and the edition. An introduction states only what its cited sources (the owner's books, or web sources named in the work's `README.md`) support, and goes live after the owner has read it.
- **A work's page:** the author and ⓘ About on one line under the title; a reading plan shows its progress as a bar with "26 / 30" (no instruction sentence); tapping a day's number marks it complete, tapping again marks it not complete, as "Mark vishram complete" at its end does. **A plan names its days by its own word** (`plans[].unit`: the Mānas's Vishram / विश्राम, a Saptāha's Day / दिन) everywhere: the work's page, the reader's footer, the player, search, the end of a day ("Day 3 complete"); never another work's word, and no deity in the message.
- **Resume follows the reading, not the screen** (the reading anchor): calm reading moves it; a fling, another section or day, search, a bookmark or Back does not, until the new place has been read (20 calm seconds with two verses read forward, or 45 calm seconds), or a recording is played there: pressing play is reading, and while it plays the recited verse is it. Resume on the work's page names the place and the one before it; in the reader, "Back to …" (laid out like the recording's "Go to …": an arrow pointing the way, the place, the part or day) shows when it is off the screen, was read in the last 15 minutes, no recording plays and Audio timing is off. Built for readers who lose their place by mistake.
- **Search looks where the reader is** (the owner): opened from a work's home it searches the whole work; opened from the reader, only what the reader shows: its part (Bal Kand), its plan day (Vishram 3) or its group (Bhagat Kabir Ji's verses), or in a work read as one part, the section on screen (Namaskarashtak). Two chips above the others say which, "Bal Kand" and "Whole work"; one tap widens it.
- **A number in a work's search finds by number:** by the verse's citation, so what the player shows finds that verse: in a work counted by dohas, "45" (or "दोहा ४५") is Doha 45 of each part (with 45क, 45ख) and "45.3" the verse cited so under it; "2.1.1" the Kaṭha's mantra, "2.1" the first mantra under it; a type's name narrows it ("chhand 10.1", "मंत्र 1.1.5"); "13a" finds 13अ.
- **The player over other screens:** while it plays, every screen keeps room at its foot for the player, so its last rows scroll clear of it.
- **YouTube that is slow to start** is never called "not allowed": the player is kept, with a note and "Play on YouTube from this verse" over it until it answers. Only YouTube's own refusal (errors 101, 150, 153) says the recording plays only on YouTube.
- **A new reader starts in English** (interface and English meaning).
- **Personal data stays on the phone** (localStorage `manas.personal.v1`): one reader per device; "Save my file" / "Open a file" on Bookmarks & notes.
- **A personal source stays on the phone too** (the owner: "This app is only for my personal use"): a meaning or commentary that is not ours to publish (Prof. Sahib Singh's Darpan for the Granth) is made in the private `scriptures-data`, never here, as a file `{"kind": "scriptures-source", …}` (`docs/LIBRARY.md` §3) and opened with "Open a file". The app keeps it in that phone's IndexedDB, puts it first among the work's meanings in its language (the default there) with that meaning turned on, and lists it in ☰ › More settings › Sources with "Remove from this phone". Nothing of it reaches the site, the library or a saved personal file.

## 7. The app
**Consistency**
- **One word for one thing**, in every label, toast, tour step and help line: verse / पद (never "text" for a verse); work / ग्रन्थ; meaning / अर्थ (never "translation"); note, bookmark, file, recording, section, library, shelf; play / सुनें (never "listen" or "hear"); letters / अक्षर (only for size); Audio timing / ऑडियोका समय; edition / संस्करण (the book a text is from); a day of a plan by the plan's own word (Vishram / विश्राम, Day / दिन). A new thing gets one word, added here; the build refuses an English label that says "translation", "colophon", or, as a short label, "listen", "hear" or "text". A verse type declared by several works has one name and tag in all of them (a stanza of an āratī, `pad`, is Stanza / पद; `shanti` is Shanti Mantra / शान्तिमन्त्र); the build refuses two.
- **Each setting has one place**, and the same control looks the same everywhere.
- **One interface language at a time**, headings, labels and numbers included.

**Header and menus**
- **One header on every page.** Left: the way back only (breadcrumbs on the library, shortened to one row; "‹ Library" on a work; ‹ in the reader). Right: A− / A+, the language or meaning capsule, search, ☰.
- **Language and meaning are one choice.** On the library and work pages, हिं · En sets the interface and the meaning together. In the reader the capsule holds the meaning switches, शब्द (word meanings) and 📖 (recitation mode); turning English on makes the interface English, only Hindi makes it Hindi. A work with a third meaning language (ਪੰ) shows two of them in the capsule, chosen under ☰ › More settings › Sources. The capsule shows a switch only for a meaning the work has: two at most, one where the work has one, none where it has none yet (Sri Guru Granth Sahib, until its Punjabi meanings come).
- **Recitation mode (📖)** hides every meaning and remembers which were on; tapping हिं or En ends it and shows only the meaning tapped (the owner: show what is clicked); 📖 again brings back the meanings that were on. The verses keep the reader's letter size (A−/A+) in every mode: switching modes never changes it.
- **One ☰ sheet everywhere:** Bookmarks & notes (in a work), Dark theme (on = dark), Share the app (the owner: one short line and one link, `<site>/get`, through the phone's share sheet; `/get` sends an Android phone to the store app, `library/apps.json` → `store`, anything else to the website; chat apps show a card: the name, the library's contents in Hindi then English, and the wide picture `tools/store/icons/share-card.png` made by `make_icons.py`), Take the tour, More settings (the owner's name for it; और सेटिंग). **More settings** holds the rare things: Transliteration, Sources, Audio timing, Privacy. While Audio timing is on, its switch and "Clear adjustments" also show at the top of ☰. Nothing in ☰ repeats the header.
- **The ⋮ menu of a verse:** Play from here · Write a note · (with a recording) Review audio timing (once marked: a blue dot, as on the verse's ▶; tap again to unmark) · Copy verse and meaning (and, only for a verse the reader corrected before, Remove my correction). Items are short commands without "the".
- **No editing in the app** (the owner: no one edits, and taking a reader's edits into the library is cumbersome): no button offers to improve a verse or a meaning or to add or edit a word's meaning (`EDITS` in `app.js` is off; the code stays). A correction a reader saved before still shows, and can be removed.
- **A tapped word's sheet:** the word with its language in brackets beside it (वाग्विदां (Sanskrit)), then its parts, its meaning (Punjabi where it has one, Hindi, English), and last, for the advanced reader, its root and grammar.

**Reader**
- **Reading comes first:** simple, clear, nothing pulls attention from the verse. Light theme by default.
- **The verse** is bold and centred; its meanings are centred under it, full width; with Hindi and English both on, English is lighter. Word meanings under a verse stay left-aligned. Footnotes are content and sit under their verse. A commentary is left-aligned, a little smaller, after the meanings, under its name.
- **The verse type is a heading** ("॥ चौपाई ॥", in saffron) where the type changes, never a corner tag.
- **The verse's corners never touch its words:** ▶ (saffron, filled) top left; bookmark (blue, filled saffron once set) top right; ⋮ (blue) bottom right; ↶ (saffron, timing mode only) bottom left; outside timing mode, for a reader who turned on "Review timing", a blue ⚑ there instead (while playing, on the verse being recited and its neighbours; always once marked).
- **Continuous scrolling** through a part or a day; at the end, one button to the next.
- **The lit verse's highlight** never crosses the previous verse's rule.
- **The footer shows where you are:** the verse range ("Dohā 29–30 ▾") and the part or day. A tap opens the section picker: tapping a part or day at the top only lists its sections; nothing opens until a section is tapped.
- **Numbers are 1 2 3 everywhere, in Hindi too** (the owner: few read १ २ ३ today): verse numbers (॥1॥), meanings, labels, times. The texts keep their own digits; the app writes each number on the page in Latin digits as it shows it. A digit inside a word (the pluta ३ of ओ३म्) stays, and so does what a textarea holds, so a correction is saved as written.
- **Nothing is wider than the screen**, even with the phone's text zoom; headings and the transliteration grow with A− / A+.
- **Vedic accents must be unmistakable:** a verse with svaras is set in Tiro Devanagari Sanskrit.

**Playing**
- **▶ on every part, every plan day and every verse;** "Play all" on a work's home. Playing is hands-free: the verse being recited stays at the top (while a section's first verse is recited, the section's blue title and any heading above it stay over it, so there is time to read them, unless they would push the verse's start under the player), the page follows it, meanings follow the switches, the screen stays on. A verse taller than the screen is followed inside too: when the line being recited (guessed from the time spent in the verse, shared among its lines by length) runs under the player, it comes up just below the bar, with its meaning when line by line — once for each line, so the reader can still scroll back by hand.
- **Playing ends where its button says:** ▶ on a part or day, and "Play from here", end with what is open ("<part> finished" and "▶ Play on"); "Play all" ends with "Recitation complete". The end message names no deity.
- **A touch or scroll never pauses the following;** when the lit verse is off screen, a pill "↑ Go to <verse>" brings it back.
- **The playing speed** (0.5–2×, YouTube's own) is a button in the player bar; ✕ is alone in its top-right corner.
- **The player never shows a recording's own title:** it shows the verse and the reading, then the reciter.

**Capsules (the messages that float over a page)**
- **Two kinds, each with one meaning.** The **dark capsule** (`toast`: ink on the page colour) answers a tap: what was just done ("Bookmarked", "Copied", "Speed 1.25×") or why it could not be ("Play first — then tap…"); it has no button and goes by itself in about 3 s. The **white capsule** (white, blue words, a soft shadow) is the reading speaking to you: where it ended ("Bal Kand finished · ▶ Play on", "Recitation complete", "Tour complete — …") or where your place is ("↑ Go to Verse 106.3", "↓ Back to …"), with a way on where there is one; it stays longer, and a tap on its button or on it acts. A new message chooses by that meaning, never by looks.

**Tour and navigation**
- **The tour is done, not read:** a welcome card, then the reader is taken into the Sundarakāṇḍa and asked to do each thing for real, with that spot lit (open the text, tap a word, drag the drawer down, A+, A−, switch a meaning, recitation mode, a bookmark, ▶). Every step after the first has "‹ Back"; "Skip" at every step; after ▶ plays 5 s, the library opens with "Tour complete — we hope you enjoy the app!". It is in every ☰ as "Take the tour".
- **Back retraces exactly, and a refresh stays on the same page** (every page and shelf is a browser-history entry; the reader's entry holds its plan, day and verse). "‹ Library" opens the library itself. Back with a drawer open only closes the drawer. A verse opened from search or a bookmark opens in the plan chosen on the work's home, on its day.
- **The headings above a verse** (the part, the topic, ॥ चौपाई ॥) come into view with it, below the top bar, whenever the reader opens or jumps to it.
- **Drawers close by dragging them down.** Buttons on a page stand 10 px apart.
- **Bookmarks & notes** lists fold under their headings (the fold is remembered); each bookmark has its own ✕.
- **Search forgives spelling** (the owner: one should not have to try a few spellings), in a work and in the library (सुंदरकांड and Sunderkand find सुन्दरकाण्ड). The text and what is typed are folded to one sound key: in Devanagari (and Gurmukhi, read as Devanagari, so Hindi letters find the Sukhmani Sahib) ि/ी, ु/ू, ब/व, श/ष/स, ण/न, ृ/रि, ै/अइ, ौ/अउ, a nasal before a consonant, ँ ं ़ ् and Vedic accents count as one; in Roman letters long vowels, aspiration, sh/s, ch/c, v/w/b, f/p, z/j, a nasal before a consonant and a word's final a; in both, a word's final u (तनु धनु धामु, काजु). A verse is found by what was typed with spaces ignored and about one letter in six allowed to differ (Tulsīdās's ऐसेउ for "aise", काजु for काज), or by most of its words in any order; the closest comes first, then meanings, then the looser matches. A Roman-letter search shows the verse itself, from its line holding the most of what was typed. **In Gurbani** (a work in Punjabi) a word's last sihari, as its last aunkar, is grammar and is folded away too (ਹਰ ਹਰ ਨਾਮ finds ਹਰਿ ਹਰਿ ਨਾਮੁ), and a final i in Roman letters. **First letters** find a line, as Gurbani is usually looked up: one word of three or more letters without vowel signs, or single letters with spaces between, are taken as the first letters of a line's words (ਸਨਕਪ, ਸ ਨ ਕ ਪ or snkp for ੴ ਸਤਿ ਨਾਮੁ ਕਰਤਾ ਪੁਰਖੁ; ੴ is passed over; the vowel carriers by family: ੳ, ਅ, ੲ). In Gurbani a line beginning with four or more of them comes first; elsewhere, and with three, after the verses holding what was typed as it is. **Names open what they name:** a part or a group (Raag Asa, ਆਸਾ, kabir, tegh bahadur) typed into a work's whole-work search is offered above the results, and a tap opens it. **A page's number** (Ang 14, ਅੰਗ ੧੪, 14) finds the verse that page begins in, then the verses beginning on it.
- **Offline:** a work opened once is readable offline with its meanings (a big work loaded by part: each part opened once); library files are fetched fresh when online (2.5 s wait) and from the phone's copy when not.

## 8. Audio
- **Recordings play from YouTube;** the library stores only verse times. The player stays visible at YouTube's minimum (200×200): no hidden audio, it breaks YouTube's terms.
- **The pipeline:** the owner's audio file → a speech-quality copy in `scriptures-data` → `source.json` + `recordings.json` → transcription (the `transcribe` workflow of this repository, free, which reads each recording from `scriptures-data` with a read-only token and keeps only the transcript; or `tools/audio/transcribe_onnx.py` with the Whisper model from GitHub) → `tools/audio/make_timings.py`. Steps: `docs/HOW-TO.md`.
- **Timing method:** `tools/audio/METHOD.md`: Whisper places, MMS times starts and ends, automatic checks flag and fix what is out of place, the owner's ear wins. A verse that overruns into the next is the next verse's start being late (the app lights a verse until the next starts).
- **A forced aligner beats the transcript match:** Whisper recognises speech; over singing it loses lines, and a lost line's start is a guess. Meta's MMS aligner, run on the known text, put 78% of the Mānas's starts within 1 s of the owner's 637 set by ear, against 46% for the transcript match (`tools/works/manas/audio/kamlesh-upadhyay/aligned.md`). A long recording whose starts matter gets MMS starts (`aligned.json`; the run: `experiments/align/` in `scriptures-data`), its lean against the owner's starts removed and its unsure starts (score below 0.5) left to the transcript and listed to check. It switches once scored better on the owner's starts by ear, or earlier on his word, published for review (`tools/audio/METHOD.md`). **A new recording does not wait for the owner's ear** (the owner: "Don't wait for me to review the recordings. Just do it using our audio algorithm"): it goes through Whisper, MMS and the automatic checks and is published; what the checks flag stays on the to-check list, and a doubtful word in the sung text is decided by the session (§2).
- **A verse the recording does not sing gets no time:** no start is guessed for it (`source.json` → `"fill": 0` when every other verse is heard), so the light stays on the verse being sung.
- **Check the timings against the transcript before publishing:** the first and last verse of each recording, and boundaries inside long breaths. Also the starts that make a verse far shorter or longer than the reciter's pace: with `"align": "anchors"` a verse whose opening repeats an earlier line's (तस्मिन् प्रतिहतेऽस्त्रे तु …) or a repeated name can be matched in the wrong place. In āratīs the alignment often matches the refrain to a later repeat. A hand-checked start goes in `fixes.json` with its evidence in `fixes.md`.
- **A recording that stops before the text ends** (a jaikāra after the last verse) gets `from` / `to` in `recordings.json` and `"align": "anchors"`.
- **The highlight must never sit on words that are not being sung:** a recording of another version is a draft until the owner chooses the text; a recording of a different composition is not timed to our text.
- **Timing by ear** (☰ › More settings › Audio timing): off by default and off again 12 hours after last use; never carried by a personal file. Under each verse: "− ⏱ Starts now +   − ⏹ Ends now +", the times and whose they are, "Until next" once an end is set; ↶ Undo in the verse's corner; the step (0.1 / 0.25 / 0.5 / 1 s) is a saffron chip in the player bar, and beside it the filled saffron "Next ▸ N", both on their own line under ✕ (the owner: ✕ one line up, so they never cover the video); on a phone under 390 px, where ⏮ ▶ ⏭ 1× do not fit beside the 200-px video, 1× moves up beside ✕ (wider phones keep it after ⏭) (the owner: one tap to the next verse still to check, after the one playing, round to the first, and it plays it; hidden when none is left). You mark the verse you look at, never the lit one; a mark never scrolls the page.
- **Timing flags** (the owner: listeners hear where the recording and the verse part): "Review audio timing" (a clock icon), third in the verse's ⋮ menu, marks it; one sign then shows it in both places, a small blue dot: on the verse's ▶ (the owner: timing belongs to playing, so its mark goes on ▶, never a flag on the verse) and at the end of the menu item (the owner: the mark on the verse must visibly be the one in the menu; a dot pairs better than a ✓); tapping it again unmarks. Only a marked verse joins the to-check list, also one whose start was set, and it leaves the list (and loses the mark) once its start is set or checked (✓ Correct). Marks stay on the phone they were made on. **"Review timing"** ("Tapping the flag adds a verse to the checklist"; a switch under ☰ › More settings › Audio timing, off by default and kept on until turned off; the owner: only a reader who wants it sees it, without the timing controls): a blue waving ⚑ at the bottom left, outside timing mode, while playing on the verse being recited and the verses just before and after it (the light may already have moved on), and always on a marked verse; a tap marks the verse (filled blue; ▶ then needs no dot) or unmarks it. The settings' "To check" list includes marked verses.
- **The light waits for you:** it never moves to a verse whose start is only a guess; a set end moves it to the next verse at once. "Ends now" gives the next unset verse that end as its start. Each − / + previews 2 s while nothing is playing.
- **One order rule, in one place:** `tools/audio/order.py` and `buildAudio` in `app.js` are the same rule, so what the phone shows is what will be published; the UX test proves they agree on every recording. The rule stays as it is: replaying the owner's 82 Mānas fixes, it leaves 55 already right when he reaches them, against 53 without re-spreading guesses and 44 with no adjustment, and it never made one worse (`experiments/align/README.md` in `scriptures-data`).
- **Sending:** "Send to the library" opens a GitHub issue; the `timing` workflow (issues from the owner's accounts only: `goyaldarwin`, `darwingoyal`) applies it and fails if any other verse would change. Owner times win over everything. A live recording takes each send at once; a draft goes live with the "checked: publish it for everyone" switch.
- **A recording covers every verse timed on it:** its `first`..`last` in the timings includes the verses the owner set after its last matched one (`make_timings.py`, the app, and a build check), so at a recording's end the player never goes back to a verse on the same recording (a loop).
- **Helpers** save their marks as a file; the owner opens it and checks each mark before it is sent.
- **What you hear now wins** (the owner: "Starts now" was refused because a later verse, started by an earlier "Ends now", already began before now): "Starts now" removes, and names in its toast, any start set on this phone and not yet sent that is out of order with it; a sent start still has to be moved first.
- **A sample to check** (the owner: "suddenly the number I have to look at increases"): a source whose to-check list is a sample for scoring (`source.json` → `check_only`) keeps exactly that list; the verses the order rule moves around his times are not added to it.
- **Verifying a send:** compare the issue's JSON with `library/<path>/audio/<source>.json` (each sent time exact), read `moved`, and check the deploy run succeeded.

## 10. Engineering
- **One repository holds everything** for the website: the data, every script and expensive intermediate (transcripts, extracted text), and the docs, so a new session can continue. Shared code in `tools/site`, `tools/audio`, `tools/meanings`, `tools/works/export.py`; generated files are never edited by hand.
- **One folder per work, one format** (`tools/works/README.md`): `tools/works/<work id>/` holds `work.json`, `text.txt`, `README.md`, `reconciliation.md`, `meanings/<lang>/<source>.json` and `commentary/<lang>/<source>.json` (any number of sources per language), `audio/<source>/` (any number of recordings), the work's own word senses in `glossary/<lang>.json`, and the scripts that make its text in `source/`. One exporter makes every work; a work that repeats another's verses takes them by id (`[take <work> …]`), never by copy. When a work needs something the format lacks, the format grows for every work (the exporter and `tools/works/README.md` together), not a script of its own.
- **Shared code goes first, on its own pull request;** check `docs/LIBRARY.md` and the app for an existing feature before writing one.
- **`build.py` refuses an inconsistent library** (every exported work, on the shelf or not yet: a branch's new work meets every check before it goes up) and an `app.js` that does not parse; the offline bundle carries only the works on the shelf. Before every commit: `python3 tools/site/check.py`, which runs the build, then side by side `regen_check.py` (each work's text generator makes exactly its committed `text.txt`, and library/ equals what `tools/works/export.py` and `make_timings.py` make, in an empty folder), `history_check.py` (no work or verse id that readers' data points at has gone), `smoke_test.py` and `ux_test.py` in shards (a few minutes; `--fast` runs the first three only, for a data change, with the browser tests left to CI; `--works a,b` adds the smoke test and those works' per-work UX checks); it gates on their exit codes, never on a grep of their output. **Checks are quick to run again:** `regen_check.py` makes again only the works whose inputs or outputs changed since its last pass on that machine (a hash of each work's folder, the folders it takes from, the shared scripts and its library folder; the CI always runs `--full`), independent works export side by side, and a browser test waits for what it needs to see, never a fixed time.
- **A generated source file is changed only in its generator:** an editorial choice about a generator's output (the plain English of the Mānas's Gita Press headings, `tools/works/manas/source/plain_en.json`) is data the generator reads, so running it again keeps the choice; `regen_check.py` reruns every text generator and fails on a difference. After merging, check that the `check` run on `main` is green (failed checks are annotations: `gh api repos/goyaldarwin/scriptures/check-runs/<job id>/annotations`).
- **A test that touches the player starts from a quiet player** (`stopAudio(); ytOK = false`). On the CI runner YouTube loads for real and moves the lit verse at any moment, so a test that taps a player button pins the verse as the tap begins and reads the result right after the app's handler (click listeners, removed afterwards), never after a wait. Tests that sample live data exclude verses set by ear; the timing tests clear the published marks in memory first, so they never depend on how far the owner has marked a recording.
- **Everything that broke gets a test** in the same change as its fix: a button is tapped the way a finger does (Playwright's `click`, which refuses a hidden or covered element), not called from code.
- **Releases must not break phones with the old version cached:** asset URLs carry a content hash; the service worker revalidates; think of readers on the previous release when moving a data file (`smoke_test.py` opens a work loaded by part with the previous release's `app.js`). A new manifest key never reuses an old one's name with another type (`split` is the Mānas's `[position, items added]` pairs, so loading by part is `load`). An element the previous release's app.js writes to stays in the page (hidden, empty) until a later release.
- **A phone never downloads a big work whole:** a work whose meanings or own word senses run to many MB is loaded by part (`work.json` → `"load": "parts"`, `docs/LIBRARY.md` §2): the verses at once, each part's meanings and senses when that part is opened.
- **The page is never machine-translated** (the owner: no "Translate this page?" bar): `translate="no"` on `<html>` and `<meta name="google" content="notranslate">`; the app has its own Hindi and English.
- **An element id is used once** in the page: `$('#id')` binds the first, so a second element with the same id takes the first one's tap away (`ux_test.py` checks it).
- **A handler that calls a function with optional arguments is wrapped** (`() => f()`), so the click event is never passed as an argument.
- **A scripted docs edit asserts its anchor first:** `str.replace` silently does nothing when the anchor is missing.
- **Merging:** large work (a new work, a shared-code change) on a branch and pull request, merged by the session once the tests pass; a small fix the owner asks for may go to `main` after the same tests. Always `git pull` first: the `timing` workflow pushes to `main`.
- **GitHub Actions minutes:** jobs in the public repo are free, so transcription runs there (its `transcribe` workflow: the recording is fetched onto the job's machine and never committed, uploaded or printed; `scriptures-data` stays private, never made public even for a while); jobs in the private `scriptures-data` (experiments, and its own `transcribe` workflow) use the owner's 2,000 included minutes a month, and more is billed. Whisper small transcribes this chant at about 1× real time on a private-repo machine. Before a private-repo run, estimate its minutes (jobs × hours each); ask the owner first when it is large (an MMS alignment of the whole Mānas takes about 2,350). The account runs at most 20 jobs at once across its repositories, so `transcribe` runs at most 14 in parallel: a long run that took all 20 left the checks, the site's publishing and the owner's timing submissions queued for hours.
- **This environment:** never `pkill -f` or `kill $(pgrep -f …)` (they kill the session's own shell; kill by PID). GitHub GraphQL is unavailable; use the REST API (`gh api …`). Hugging Face is blocked; the Whisper model comes from GitHub (`tools/audio/transcribe_onnx.py`). Tesseract `hin`/`mar`/`san` install with apt.

## 11. Extraction techniques
- **No OCR** (the owner: OCR leads to errors). A text is read from the PDF's text layer: its codes or glyph ids decoded with a complete map, every code confirmed by its glyph shape (rendered from the embedded font) and by the words it occurs in, an unknown code stops the decoding, and every word is checked to be well-formed. The checks that replace OCR: the well-formedness scan, the metre, words found nowhere else in the work, comparison with witnesses, and the page image read by eye where a check flags a place.
- **A scan with no text layer the owner chose to OCR** (the Śiva Purāṇa's Hindi: `tools/works/shiva-purana/source/hindi/`): two Tesseract passes (`hin`, `Devanagari`) merged word by word; a word they read differently, or that the word list lacks, is read by eye on a crop of its printed line, with the reader's whole line kept to set right a word both passes misread alike; a two-column page is read in bands cut where a line crosses the column rule (fitted down a leaning page); the walk through the adhyāyas follows the printed closing lines; footnotes, running heads, headings and subtitles are kept out of the paragraphs. Then every paragraph is proofread against its page: words both passes misread alike are not flagged (about one paragraph in ten held one), so the flagged-word reading alone is not enough.
- **Gita Press Hindi PDFs** have a broken text layer: decode from glyph ids (PyMuPDF `get_texttrace()`) with a glyph → Unicode map (pre-base ि, reph combinations, conjuncts, nukta forms, empty placeholder glyphs; after a halant a zero-advance space means ZWNJ, between consonants a missing ्). Where the font is an open one (Noto Sans Devanagari), map each glyph by shaping the official font and confirm the outlines match (`tools/works/valmiki-ramayana/source/hindi/`).
- **Gita Press Sanskrit in the legacy Chanakya font** (the Vālmīki Rāmāyaṇa): the text layer gives each glyph as the Mac Roman character of its code; `tools/works/valmiki-ramayana/source/chanakya.py` maps all 171 codes and puts the pieces in Unicode order.
- **Gita Press English PDFs** mix fonts: Devanagari in a legacy 8-bit font (Chanakya/Kruti style), transliteration in a custom encoding (å=ā, ∂=ī …). Convert, then check by turning the transliteration back into Devanagari and diffing.
- **Footnotes run across pages** (one starting lowercase continues the previous) and restart their marks per page: match notes to marks in order. A note is one paragraph: its printed lines are joined (a new note starts with its mark); a line-end hyphen stays in a hyphenated word (forty-eight) and goes where the printer split a word (expand-ing), each such hyphen decided by hand (`tools/works/manas/source/make.py`).
- **Web-page printouts** often have a broken Devanagari text layer: OCR the page images, or read the text layer by position (`pdftotext -bbox`) where an overlay hides lines.
- **A book whose Devanagari is in a legacy font** can still be the source through its IAST layer, converted letter for letter and checked against another witness.
- **A śānti mantra printed again at the end of an Upaniṣad is not an item:** the work ends with its last verse (the Kaṭha ends with 2.3.18).
- **A video's on-screen text** can be the source when the owner says so: crop the subtitle band at full width, transcribe line by line with the frame number; repeated screenshots of a line are one line. Where the video's Devanagari and its transliteration disagree, follow the correct one.
